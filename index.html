About Us

A Ghanaian non-governmental organisation devoted to the reproductive and maternal well-being of girls, women and pregnant women.

Our Story

Time with Midwife Safia was founded on a simple belief: when girls and women understand their bodies and their rights, families and communities thrive.

Rooted in the day-to-day experience of midwifery, we bring clear, compassionate and practical health education to places where information is hard to find, and we speak up for the policies and services women deserve.

We work through workshops, school sessions, antenatal talks, community dialogues and media campaigns, always in a warm, respectful and stigma-free way.

Our Mission

To educate and empower girls, women and pregnant women with accurate reproductive and maternal health knowledge, and to advocate for accessible, quality and respectful care across Ghana.

Our Vision

A Ghana where every girl grows up informed, every woman makes confident choices about her body, and every pregnancy and birth is safe and dignified.

Meet Midwife Safia

Midwife Safia is a dedicated health professional whose years at the bedside shaped this organisation. Having supported women through pregnancy, childbirth and recovery, she saw first-hand how a lack of information can put mothers and babies at risk.

Today she leads a growing team of volunteers, health workers and community champions committed to making reliable knowledge available to every girl and woman.

Health Education

Simple, trustworthy guidance on reproductive and maternal health. Tap a topic to learn more.

Menstrual health & puberty
  • Menstruation is a normal, healthy part of growing up, with no shame attached.
  • Change pads regularly and wash hands and the body with clean water.
  • Cycles can be irregular in the first years. See a health worker if periods are extremely painful, very heavy or suddenly stop.
Healthy pregnancy & antenatal care
  • Start antenatal visits as early as possible and attend all scheduled appointments.
  • Eat a balanced diet with iron-rich foods, and take supplements prescribed by your health worker.
  • Sleep under an insecticide-treated net and avoid herbal or other medicines unless advised by a professional.
Pregnancy danger signs: go to a facility immediately
  • Vaginal bleeding or fluid leaking from the vagina
  • Severe headache, blurred vision or swelling of face and hands
  • Fever, severe abdominal pain or convulsions
  • Baby moving much less than usual

These can be life-threatening. Do not wait. Seek skilled care straight away.

Preparing for a safe delivery
  • Plan where you will deliver and how you will get there, including transport and funds.
  • Choose a skilled birth attendant at a health facility.
  • Pack essentials early and agree on a support person.
After birth: mother & baby
  • Attend postnatal checks for both mother and baby.
  • Breastfeed exclusively for the first six months where possible.
  • Keep the baby's immunisation schedule and seek help for low mood, which is common and treatable.
Family planning & reproductive choices
  • Every woman has the right to information about safe, effective methods.
  • Spacing pregnancies gives the body time to recover and improves outcomes for mother and baby.
  • A health worker can help you choose a method that fits your health and life.
Important: This page offers general education and is not a substitute for personal medical advice. If you are pregnant or unwell, please visit a qualified health professional or your nearest health facility.

Health Tools

Free, private calculators and trackers to help girls and women understand their bodies and plan healthier pregnancies. Nothing you enter is saved or sent anywhere.

Period & Ovulation Calculator

Predict your next periods, ovulation day and fertile window.

Estimates only, and cycles can vary. This is not a method of birth control.

Due Date & Pregnancy Week

Find your estimated due date, current week and suggested antenatal visit dates.

An ultrasound scan or your midwife can confirm your exact due date.

Baby Kick Counter

From about 28 weeks, tap each time you feel your baby move and see how long it takes to reach 10.

Tap to start the timer

Important: if your baby moves less than usual, or you are worried, contact your midwife or go to a health facility right away.

BMI Calculator

Check your body mass index (for adults aged 18 and above).

BMI is a general guide only. During pregnancy, healthy weight gain is assessed by your midwife.

Daily Water Intake

A simple estimate of how much water you should drink each day.

Needs rise with hot weather and exercise. Ask a health worker if you have a medical condition.

Hospital Bag Checklist

Tick items as you pack them. Start from about 34 weeks.

Please note: These tools give general estimates and education only. They do not replace advice from a qualified midwife, doctor or health facility. If you feel unwell or have any pregnancy danger signs, seek care immediately.
